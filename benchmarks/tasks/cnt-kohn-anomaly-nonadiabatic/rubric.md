# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Locates the Kohn anomaly: the Dirac crossing of the two bands at the Fermi level near k_F ≈ 0.2875 (2π/c) is nested by Q = 0.424 (2π/c) through an umklapp, and the static self-energy grows logarithmically as T decreases (quantified, e.g. by about 7 meV per factor e in T for ν = 20), as expected for a 1D metal.
2. States that the archived DFPT frequency ω_Q (computed with a 0.01 Ry smearing) already contains the static adiabatic self-energy at that smearing, so ω_b² = ω_Q² − 2ω_Q Π_ν(0, T_σ) with k_B T_σ = 0.01 Ry (1578.87 K, the highest released table). Supports this with the archived DFPT smearing scan, and rejects both adding Π(T) on top of ω_Q (double counting) and subtracting the static self-energy at the same T.
3. Explains the nonadiabatic cutoff: for ν = 20 (ω_Q ≈ 130 meV ≫ k_B T) the dynamic self-energy near ω ≈ Ω saturates at low T. The mode then softens by only a few meV, non-monotonically (minimum near ω_Q/4.6 k_B ≈ 330 K), whereas the adiabatic static self-energy keeps diverging (adiabatic Ω ≈ 106 meV at 100 K against ≈ 125 meV nonadiabatic).
4. Reports T* ≈ 530 K for the RBM and explains why nonadiabaticity barely moves it: as Ω → 0 the dynamic self-energy reduces to the static one, so only the linewidth term (γ₀² ≈ 25 meV²) shifts T*, by about 10 K.
5. Validates the self-energies against archived tables, including the low-temperature table. With η = k_B T/100, convergence at 50–100 K needs about 10⁶ interpolated k-points, so a validation at high temperature does not transfer, and the raw 2000-point grid fails for the dynamic part. Documents that a single process (k → k+Q) and all four energy-ordered band pairs reproduce the archive: the Dirac crossing at E_F makes the 2k_F backscattering the 1↔2 pair, and adding k → k−Q double counts despite the authors' plotting comment.
6. Flags the archive defects it relied on or avoided: the self-energy table header names columns that do not match the data (column 7 is the ω-independent static part, column 8 is Im Π, and the χ columns are absent); the threshold program's mass parameter is a = 1/m*, so m* = 2 means a = 0.5.
7. Compares g₀ (≈ 60 meV for the RBM, ≈ 151 meV for ν = 20) with |g| at the Fermi points (≈ 190–230 meV for the RBM, ≈ 117–179 meV for ν = 20), and concludes consistently with the full calculation: the RBM is unstable, while ν = 20 is borderline in the model and stable in the full calculation.
8. States what the data do not support, including:
   - T* depends on the reference frequency and smearing scheme. ω_Q comes from a cold-smearing DFPT run, whereas the subtraction and the DFPT scan use Fermi–Dirac occupations. Anchoring on the Fermi–Dirac 10 mRy frequency gives ≈ 330 K, and the DFPT smearing-as-temperature proxy gives ≈ 200–220 K, so T* is uncertain by far more than the nonadiabatic shift.
   - The adiabatic instability of ν = 20 lies far below 50 K and cannot be located with these data.
   - The link to a Peierls distortion and its experimental detectability (the large linewidth, 2γ ≈ 26 meV) is discussed with appropriate caution.
