# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Obtains each chemical potential from electron counting in the 24-orbital spinless model (24 valence electrons, 12 filled bands, exact electron–hole compensation), and notes that the archived `E_FERMI` values are not the neutral points: the ZrSiS `input.dat` is a copy of the ZrSiSe file (Se atoms, ZrSiSe cell, same E_FERMI, about 0.78 eV below the ZrSiS neutral point), ZrSiSe's E_FERMI lies about 10 meV below neutrality (n_e/n_h ≈ 0.7) and ZrSiTe's about 35 meV above it (n_e/n_h ≈ 1.9); also that `NumOccupied = 13` in the ZrSiTe input is inconsistent with the count.
2. Explains the non-saturating magnetoresistance by electron–hole compensation, and shows that breaking the compensation (archived E_FERMI) makes MR(B ∥ b) saturate (exponent ≈ 0.3 instead of ≈ 1.6), so the field dependence is a test of the carrier balance.
3. Shows that the field-dependence and angular results are converged in the k mesh, and recognises that a coarse mesh mimics physical effects: the 40 mesh fakes saturation for B ∥ b and moves the butterfly maximum to ≈ 35°.
4. Links the butterfly maximum (≈ 55° from c at Bτ = 5 T ps) to the anisotropic electron–hole compensation of closed orbits on the concave pockets, not to open orbits: the thermal-weighted open-orbit fraction is below 1% for all bc-plane directions at the neutral point. Explains why a closed-orbit effective-mass (two-band Drude) model gives a monotonic pattern with its maximum at an end point.
5. States that the semiclassical calculation contains no Berry phase or other topological information, so the butterfly pattern needs neither nodal-line topology nor a topological transition; and that these data cannot test SOC gapping (the models have no SOC).
6. Contrasts ZrSiTe: different pockets (an extra electron band, band 14), a peanut-shaped pattern with the maximum at B ∥ c and a nearly saturating MR for B ∥ b; notes that the ZrSiTe Hamiltonian is not symmetrized, so ρ_xx(θ) ≠ ρ_xx(−θ) in the model, an artefact rather than physics.
7. Compares the two samples at the Bτ of each (5 T ps for ZrSiSe, 2 T ps for ZrSiTe), not at equal Bτ, and explains Kohler scaling with one relaxation time.
8. Reports that the Hall resistivity of the compensated crystals is a small difference of electron and hole terms that is not converged in the mesh, while at the archived (uncompensated) E_FERMI values ρ_yx changes sign near Bτ ≈ 5 T ps; so a Hall sign change is not a robust signature of a particular mechanism.
