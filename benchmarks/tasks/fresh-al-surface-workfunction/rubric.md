# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Defines σ = (E_slab − N ε_bulk)/2A with both surfaces counted, and explains that ε_bulk must be sampled exactly like the slab (same in-plane mesh and smearing, for example an oriented bulk cell with a dense kz). Otherwise σ_N drifts linearly with N (Boettger's divergence). Notes that a straight-line fit of E_N over a short window of thicknesses is biased by the quantum-size oscillations, and shows this from its own data (window dependence, or the fitted slope differing from the oriented bulk energy).
2. Notes that thick metallic slabs need more Kohn–Sham bands than the pw.x default: with the default, the highest band falls below the Fermi level for the thicker slabs, the SCF needs many more iterations and σ_N jumps upward, which mimics a divergent surface energy. Shows that it checked the band count (highest band against E_F) or ran with enough bands.
3. Defines φ = V_vac − E_F with V_vac taken from the plateau of the planar-averaged electrostatic (ionic plus Hartree) potential, and checks the plateau and the vacuum convergence. Explains why the total local potential, which also contains exchange–correlation, gives a vacuum level about 0.2 eV too low that does not converge to the electrostatic one as the vacuum grows.
4. Identifies quantum-size oscillations of σ_N and φ_N with N, with amplitudes of order 0.01 J/m² and a few hundredths of an eV, and with a longer-period drift of φ(100) between N ≈ 7–9 and N ≥ 10. Handles them by averaging over a range of thick slabs, and states the residual uncertainty. Does not converge thickness by comparing two thin slabs.
5. States the work-function ordering φ(100) > φ(111) by about 0.2 eV, a difference much larger than the quantum-size scatter, so Al violates the Smoluchowski expectation for a close-packed face, consistent with experiment. Explains the surface dipole and Smoluchowski smoothing.
6. Reports the outward expansion of d12 of Al(111), about 1 %, from symmetric relaxations of slabs thick enough (thin slabs give about half the value). Notes that the unrelaxed slabs already show outward forces on the surface planes. Notes that a one-sided relaxation needs the dipole correction, or both vacuum levels, to avoid a dipole error in φ.
7. States limitations: PBE underestimates surface energies relative to experiment; σ and φ refer to bulk-terminated slabs at a fixed lattice constant; the thick-slab predictions carry the residual quantum-size uncertainty. Does not over-interpret differences below the convergence scatter.
