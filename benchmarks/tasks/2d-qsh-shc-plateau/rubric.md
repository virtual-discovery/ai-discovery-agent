# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Separates topology from the size of the spin Hall response: establishes Z2 from the Wilson-loop (hybrid Wannier centre) flow of the occupied bands, counted as electrons minus excluded bands, and states that Bi2In2 is Z2-trivial despite a large in-gap σ^z_xy (≈0.8), while Bi2 is Z2-odd with a plateau well below 1 and Li2Tl2 is Z2-odd with a plateau far above 1.
2. Explains the deviations from quantization by the non-conservation of S_z: only CuLi2As (horizontal mirror, D3h) conserves S_z and gives |σ^z_xy| = 1; in the other Z2-odd models spin–orbit mixing makes the plateau non-universal, and its magnitude is not fixed by Z2.
3. Places the chemical potential inside the gap of the archived Wannier model, after showing that the scf Fermi level (cold smearing) lies inside the valence band of the small-gap models, that the `.win` fermi_energy is the valence-band maximum, and that for Bi2In2 the `.win` band edges come from the coarse scf grid while the Wannier valence-band maximum at a generic k lies above the `.win` mid-gap.
4. Converts the Wannier90 output, which uses the vacuum-padded cell volume, to a sheet conductance with each material's own cell height c, and states the unit (ħ/e)(e²/h) = e/2π.
5. Demonstrates k-mesh convergence where the gap is small (Ti2I2N2 at about 22 meV, Li2Tl2 at about 31 meV): coarse meshes alias the spin Berry curvature hot spots, and dense or adaptively refined meshes are needed; the dependence on the broadening η is small inside the gap.
6. Identifies that placing every Wannier function at its lattice point makes the response violate the crystal symmetry (Bi2 then shows σ^x_xy, which the mirror x → −x forbids) and uses the Wannier-centre position operator; relates the plateau spin direction to symmetry (Bi2: σ^y_xy allowed and σ^x_xy forbidden; Hg4O2, space group P1: all components allowed, spin canted far from z).
7. Recognises that the archived `Top_materials` spin Hall files and the paper's table describe relaxed structures with different lattice constants, not the archived database models, and does not use them as the answer; cross-checks the invariant where the gap is small (fine Wilson-loop sampling or ribbon edge states).
8. States what the data do not support: the spin operator is the projected spinor operator (no `.spn` in the archive) and the position operator is diagonal, so non-quantized plateaus carry an approximation error; the maximally localized Wannier functions of some models (Ti2I2N2, Bi2In2) break the crystal symmetry and give small symmetry-forbidden in-plane components; DFT-PBE gaps and the dynamical stability of several candidates are not established here.
