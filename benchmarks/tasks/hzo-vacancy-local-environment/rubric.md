# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Identifies the two oxygen sublattices: threefold-coordinated (O3) and fourfold-coordinated (O4) by first-shell cations. Quantifies that an O3 vacancy costs more than an O4 vacancy with the same Zr count (PBE): about 0.27–0.28 eV with no Zr neighbours, shrinking slightly as Zr neighbours are added (0.22–0.25 eV with two or three), with about 0.25 eV in a common-slope description. Relates this to the shorter, stronger bonds of the threefold site (about 2.03–2.13 Å, against 2.11–2.25 Å for O4).
2. Quantifies the lowering of E_f per Zr first-shell neighbour (about −0.10 eV on O3 and −0.08 to −0.09 eV on O4, approximately additive), and attributes it to weaker Zr–O bonding.
3. Tests for a composition effect by comparing matched first-shell environments across compositions. Finds that E_f falls by about 0.03 eV per 0.25 increase of x at fixed environment (about −0.11 to −0.12 eV per unit x). Notes that in pooled data this shift is confounded with the Zr-count slope, because each cell's environment mix follows its composition.
4. States that the geometric descriptors of the staged structures (M–O distances, M–O–M angle groups) are identical within a sublattice. They therefore reproduce the sublattice split but cannot explain the Zr dependence: geometry and coordination cannot be separated with these data, because relaxed geometries are not released.
5. Notes that all three supercells share one lattice, so the composition shift cannot be separated from a fixed-volume strain or electrostatic effect. Does not claim a mechanism for it.
6. Obtains the populations from random-alloy (binomial) environment probabilities times Boltzmann factors. Explains why the single 96-atom decoration at fixed composition misrepresents rare environments. States that rare Zr-rich environments hold a disproportionate share of vacancies at low x (four-Zr sites are about 0.2 % of O sites but about 10 % of vacancies at x = 0.25), and that fourfold sites hold more than 99 % of vacancies.
7. Uses the CASTEP data only as relative energies within one functional, since no pristine or reference energies exist for them. Recognises that the authors' labels of each functional's calculations differ from the environments of the VASP x = 0.50 cell (separate decorations). Reports that the Zr softening is robust to the functional while the O3–O4 offset is not (about 0.12 eV in LDA against about 0.23–0.25 eV in PBE in the common-slope description), so the small O3 share of vacancies is functional-sensitive.
8. Gives uncertainties, and states what the data do not support: charged vacancies (neutral only), conclusions that need more than one decoration per composition, and supercell-size convergence, unless the 3×3×3 cell was checked; if it was, reports that its CASTEP-PBE relative energies follow the 2×2×2 trends.
