# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Converts SdH frequencies with n = e f/h per peak. Justifies this by the non-degeneracy of every pocket in the gated, inversion-asymmetric film (surface states, spin-split bulk subbands, hole pockets), and checks it against the Hall or gate charge balance rather than assuming 2e f/h for bulk bands.
2. Distinguishes subband peaks from harmonics and sum frequencies of the strong surface-state peaks, using their gate-voltage dependence across several gate voltages (ridge tracking) and/or the model, not single-spectrum peak strength. States that the hole-pocket ridge is weaker than harmonics at single gate voltages. On the electron side, identifies the first bulk subband as the ridge that starts from zero density near its onset (about 0 V), and the top surface as the steep ridge that is continuous with the surface-state densities across neutrality; a flat feature at twice the bottom-surface density is a harmonic, even though it can satisfy the Hall sum rule.
3. Identifies the top (gate-side) surface as the state whose density follows the gate, on both sides of charge neutrality. Explains that the bottom surface is screened by the top surface and the bulk, so its density stays nearly constant; a rigid-band (non-self-consistent) filling would wrongly charge it.
4. Explains why a self-consistent full-band Hartree treatment (charge neutrality defined over the whole inverted band structure) is needed, rather than rigid-band filling or effective-mass screening, using the near-constant bottom-surface density as evidence.
5. Explains the hole-pocket analysis of the wedge runs: kdotpy extrapolates a partial kphi range to the full circle, so the wedge IDOS must be divided by 360°/width, and it must be read at the main run's Fermi level, not at the wedge run's own E_F. Shows that the full band −1 equals 2 × (majority + minority) pocket.
6. States that bulk inversion asymmetry splits the four valence-band side maxima into two inequivalent pairs, so the majority pocket has multiplicity 2. Supports this with charge balance (holes = surface electrons − net density).
7. States that the hole pockets start to fill on the electron side of the charge-neutrality point (semimetallic overlap: holes coexist with surface electrons), so neither the CNP nor the Hall sign change marks the hole onset.
8. Compares model and experiment quantitatively: the top surface and the first bulk subband agree; the bottom surface (model too high) and the majority hole pocket (model too low) disagree. Names candidate causes (interface or trap charge and boundary potential, dielectric screening, VBO/E_P, a hole-side lever-arm change) without claiming that one archived parameter set can separate them.
