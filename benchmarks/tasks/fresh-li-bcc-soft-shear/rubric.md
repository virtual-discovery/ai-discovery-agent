# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Locates the protocol's own zero-pressure lattice parameter (energy minimum or zero pressure at converged settings) before straining, rather than using the experimental lattice parameter, and states that stress–strain slopes taken about a pressurized reference cell are not the zero-pressure elastic constants.
2. Shows that total energies and pressures converge on coarse k meshes while the shear response does not: C′ (a sub-0.1 meV/atom energy or ~0.05 GPa stress difference at 1 % strain) must be converged itself, with a k-mesh ladder at small smearing; recognises that the largest smearing width gives a k-converged but biased C′, and that coarse meshes at small smearing give noisy values that can even change sign.
3. Uses symmetry-adapted strains (volume-conserving or uniaxial tetragonal strains for C′ from the stress anisotropy or energy, trigonal [111] shear for C44), with the engineering-shear Voigt convention (ΔF/V = ½ C44 e4² per shear component, three components for the trigonal strain), and explains why C′ is not obtained as a small difference of separately fitted C11 and C12 or B.
4. Recognises that the Bain-path energy is strongly asymmetric (large third-order term): +e and −e strains give very different one-sided moduli, so C′ is taken from symmetric ±e differences (or a fit with odd terms) at small amplitude, with a check of the amplitude dependence.
5. Explains the finite-pressure correction in the compressed cell: energy curvatures of strains that change the volume include the −PΔV work (for linear tetragonal and trigonal strains they give B + P/2, Lagrangian-strain coefficients give B + P), whereas the stress–strain slopes and exactly volume-conserving energy curvatures give the stability coefficients B′ and B44.
6. States the Born criteria (C11 − C12 > 0, C44 > 0, C11 + 2C12 > 0; under pressure in terms of B′, B44, B) and concludes on mechanical stability, while distinguishing elastic (long-wavelength) stability from thermodynamic phase stability relative to fcc, hcp and 9R, which these calculations do not address.
7. Explains the small C′ and large anisotropy physically: the nearly free-electron Fermi surface close to the N points, whose band-structure (Fermi-surface) contribution to the tetragonal shear nearly cancels the electrostatic (Ewald) stiffness; and notes the Cauchy-relation violation (C12 ≠ C44) as a signature of non-central metallic bonding.
8. Compares with experiment (78 K: C′ ≈ 1.2 GPa, C44 ≈ 10.8 GPa, A ≈ 9) and states the limits: static lattice without zero-point or thermal expansion, one functional and pseudopotential, harmonic moduli local to the two volumes studied.
