# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. States the spin model: classical unit vectors on randomly placed dopants of both Bi layers, each pair counted once with the archived J of its shell and species pair, concentrations as fractions of Bi sites. Identifies spin-carrying dopants from the single-impurity moments (m01/m02), not from the dimer columns.
2. Notes that the archived dimer moment `m2` is impurity 2's moment only in the first shell: it is ≈0.01 μB beyond it for every pair, contradicted by the same species' `m1` in its own rows. Does not read this as moment collapse.
3. Obtains T_C from disorder-averaged finite-size scaling, e.g. crossings of Binder ratios for at least two supercell sizes with many random placements. Says how the crossing drifts with size or why the sizes used suffice.
4. Quantifies the failure of mean-field theory (multi-sublattice, or the authors' averaged estimate): a four- to tenfold overestimate here. Attributes it to dilution, since strong short-range pairs are rare and order percolates through weak long-range couplings, and to frustration.
5. Explains why V 2% + Cr 2% shows no order above 2 K although mean field predicts about 30 K, and that the ordering temperature is not proportional to concentration in this regime.
6. Explains the co-dopant ranking with specific couplings. Nb has the largest cross-coupling and mean-field T_C with Cr, but its antiferromagnetic Nb–Nb shells (6.10 Å and 7.51 Å) frustrate the network: Cr 5% + Nb 5% shows at most weak, strongly sample-dependent order (Binder ratio stays near 1.2). Mo's strong antiferromagnetic shell (6.49 Å) has only three neighbours, while its ferromagnetic Cr–Mo couplings, short- and long-range, carry the order. Contrasts this with the paper's mean-field recommendation.
7. Identifies that the archive has no Co–Co dimers within 7.8 Å, and that the 9.99 Å Co–Co run lost its moment. Reports T_C of Cr5Co5 as undetermined instead of filling the gap with zeros or guesses.
8. Tests or justifies the exchange range: the far shells (7–16, 8.8–12.2 Å) matter at these concentrations, and couplings beyond 12.2 Å are absent from the data.
9. States what the data do not support: anisotropy, DMI and quantum spin effects are absent from the classical isotropic model; dopant clustering, interstitials and carrier density in real samples are not modelled; the Curie temperatures are not predictions for measured films.
