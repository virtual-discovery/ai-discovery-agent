# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. States the energy convention used for the mapping (unit vectors, each pair counted once, single-ion term per site) and applies it consistently in the predictions.
2. Notices that the rectangular cells of VBr2, VI2 and MnCl2 are distorted: the six nearest neighbours split into four bonds (3.71, 4.02, 3.68 Å) and two bonds along b (3.77, 4.07, 3.74 Å). Measures both and finds very different exchange (about 12.3 vs 7.8 meV in VBr2, 4.0 vs 1.1 meV in VI2, 1.2 vs 1.8 meV in MnCl2), whereas MnBr2 and MnI2 are undistorted despite their rectangular cells.
3. Explains the anisotropic-triangular criterion: with interchain coupling J' on the four bonds and J along b, the classical state is a spiral along b with cos(πk) = −J'/(2J) when J' < 2J (VBr2, MnCl2, incommensurate), and the collinear stripe when J' > 2J (VI2). Assuming six equivalent bonds would give the 120° state instead.
4. Ni dihalides: ferromagnetic J1 with antiferromagnetic J3 above |J1|/4 gives an incommensurate spiral along the bond direction (q ≈ 0.08 and 0.12 of b1 + b2 for NiCl2 and NiBr2). MnBr2 and MnI2: J2/J1 exceeds 1/8 (stripe in a J1–J2 model), but the antiferromagnetic J3 stabilizes the 120° state, so J3 must be measured. VCl2 is a 120° antiferromagnet.
5. Justifies the supercells by an aliasing argument: a pair separated by δ must have no periodic image within the coupling range (for example, a 2a pair in a cell four lattice constants long is counted twice). Checks for couplings beyond the third shell and finds them at noise level.
6. VI2: finds that the stripe moments are tilted about 30° from c within the ac plane. The single-ion term alone (easy axis c), or comparing only the x, y and z directions, misses the tilt; the symmetric off-diagonal J_xz of the nearest-neighbour tensor survives the mirror average over the four bonds and produces it. Establishes this with configurations whose energy differences exceed the noise (for example, global stripe states along off-axis directions), not with single noisy four-state components.
7. Finite temperature: the exchange is isotropic to about 0.1 meV, so by Mermin–Wagner there is no long-range order at T > 0 without the anisotropy. Ordering temperatures (Ising-like for an easy axis, BKT for an easy plane, possible discrete-symmetry or Z2-vortex transitions for the frustrated states) are set logarithmically by anisotropies of 0.01–0.1 meV and cannot be given reliably from these data; mean-field estimates overestimate them.
8. States the limits: the anisotropies are at or below the source's stated precision (0.1 meV), the energies come from the authors' fitted model rather than new DFT, and quantum fluctuations and interlayer coupling are not addressed.
