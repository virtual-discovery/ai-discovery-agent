# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. States the conventions: the archived TB2J energy counts ordered pairs with unit vectors (each bond twice), stabilization energies are classical and per magnetic ion, and the FM-reference couplings are carried onto the converged supercell by lattice translation.
2. Applies the self-consistency test to every material. All FM references are unstable. The AFM references of NiO (AF-II), FeCl2 (ferromagnetic layers stacked antiferromagnetically) and FePS3 (zigzag chains, both U) are positive semi-definite with only the Goldstone zero. The archived FeP spiral moments are not a stationary point of their own exchange (nonzero torques give a spurious negative eigenvalue at Γ), and the directions must be relaxed in their exchange field before the stability test.
3. Contrasts the insulators with the metal, with numbers. In NiO and FeCl2 the FM-reference exchange reproduces the converged stabilization to 1–3 %. FePS3 at U = 0 differs by about 11 %, but its AFM calculation used a different, zigzag-distorted structure, so reference and lattice effects are confounded. In metallic FeP the spiral-reference exchange stabilizes the helix about 7–8 times more strongly: the net coupling sum changes sign and the short Fe–Fe bonds become several times more antiferromagnetic. The FM reference predicts nearly the right helix vector for FeP, but not its energetics or its intra-cell phase arrangement.
4. Establishes the exchange range needed, with a convergence test under the budget. FeP's FM-reference k_min needs couplings beyond about 15 Å (a 10–11 Å list gives a modulation along b; 14 Å is still off by about 0.01 r.l.u.). The energies converge more slowly than k_min (the 18–30 Å windows still matter for FeP). FeCl2's stacking energy and FePS3 need couplings beyond 8–11 Å.
5. Recognizes that the FeCl2 and FeP AFM supercell lists were computed with R = 0 along the long axis only. Bonds across the cell boundary are listed at the far image with long archived distances: for example, a −4.2 meV FeP bond of 3.81 Å appears at 25.7 Å, and FeCl2's interlayer bonds appear at about 30.5 Å. For energies every listed pair must be kept (skipping FeCl2's 30–40 Å window loses about 16 % of its stacking energy). For the folded k_min each pair must be moved to its minimum image. As listed, the folded exchange gives a spurious (0, 1/2, 0), and the dispersion along the helix axis is flat, as in the authors' archived FeP h(k).
6. Identifies that the archived FePS3 DFT+U FM file duplicates the U = 0 FM calculation: same cell, same moments (3.377 μB, against 3.642 μB in the DFT+U AFM run) and identical couplings. The FM-reference result at U = 3 eV is therefore not determined, and the difference between the 'DFT+U FM' and DFT+U AFM exchange is a U effect, not a reference effect.
7. Tests FeP's self-consistency quantitatively. The converged (spiral-reference) exchange has k_min ≈ (0, 0.027, 0.19), a slightly longer period than its own 1×1×5 reference (q_c = 0.2) and than the FM-reference k_min ≈ (0, 0.031, 0.218). The residual instability of the relaxed 1×1×5 spiral is a fraction of 1 meV, inside the paper's ε window, so the commensurate loop is closed but the incommensurate pitch is not resolved by the supercell data.
8. States the limits. The exchange is isotropic only (FeP's spin canting needs anisotropic exchange or DMI). No DFT total energies are available to test the mapping directly. Each reference calculation used its own relaxed structure. Transition temperatures and the true incommensurate pitch are not supported.
