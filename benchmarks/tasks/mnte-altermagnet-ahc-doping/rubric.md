# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Explains the altermagnetic symmetry: the two Mn sublattices are related by the 6₃ screw (C2z with c/2), not by a translation or inversion. For moments ±y, lists the magnetic group: E, I, C2z and m_z (with c/2) as unitary operations, and C2x, C2y (with c/2), m_x and m_y (with c/2) combined with time reversal. Concludes that only σ_xy is allowed (the Hall vector transforms like a magnetization along z), and that for moments along x the unitary C2x and C2y·t(c/2) forbid all three components.
2. Notices that the rotation matrices of `3_wannsymm/symmetries.dat` are not symmetries of the archived structure and moments (they include sixfold and threefold operations that do not map the atoms or moments onto themselves) and disagree with the operation labels in `wannsymm.out`, so they were not used for the symmetry analysis.
3. Recognises that the archived `wannier90_hr.dat` is not symmetrized (the authors' WannierTools run read a WannSymm-symmetrized file that is not archived; the symmetrized bands of `bands_symmed.dat` differ by up to ≈26 meV), and attributes the nonzero σ_yz and σ_zx of the service to this numerical symmetry breaking and to mesh noise, reporting them as zero.
4. Places the energy reference correctly: the valence-band maximum of the Wannier model (≈6.132 eV) lies off the high-symmetry lines near k ≈ (±0.02, 0, ±0.42) in reciprocal-lattice units, above the path and DFT-grid maxima; the VASP E-fermi (6.21 eV) lies in the gap. Counts holes in spinor bands (one electron per band and k-point, 22 filled bands, no extra spin factor).
5. Documents mesh convergence: coarse meshes give spikes and spurious sign changes of σ_xy from Berry-curvature hot spots where the spin–orbit-split top valence bands nearly touch; values are compared between the densest meshes with a small smearing, and the low-doping region near the band edge is identified as unconverged rather than reported.
6. States that σ_xy is odd under reversal of the Néel vector (checked with the reversed model), that opposite-Néel domains cancel in a multidomain sample while the 120°-rotated in-plane domains share the same sign, and that a net Hall signal requires a domain imbalance that the weak net moment along c (allowed by the same symmetry, ≈6×10⁻⁴ μB per cell in the DFT run) can select in a field; the moment itself is far too small to produce the Hall conductivity, which arises from the compensated order.
7. States what the data do not support: electron-doped values (on the fitting grid the Wannier conduction bands miss the DFT ones by up to ≈1.6 eV, and the Wannier conduction-band minimum lies ≈0.4 eV above the DFT one at K), converged values at realistic low hole densities, extrinsic contributions and actual domain populations, and the conductivity of the authors' symmetrized model beyond the archived output.
