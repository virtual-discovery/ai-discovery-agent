# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. States that the archived GW and BSE calculations already include SOC, with evidence: the Eo energies of the QP tables reproduce the SOC band structures and not the SOC-free ones, and the paired bands of the QP tables are split away from the zone centre. Does not add a DFT SOC correction to the archived QP gaps. Quantifies the SOC change of the minimum direct gap: about −0.28 eV for Te3, −0.09 eV for Bi2Te3 and at most −0.03 eV for S3 and As2S3. Notes that the header line "Perturbative SOC: no" does not mean that SOC is absent.
2. Identifies that the runs labelled BSE_G0W0 for Te3, As2S3 and Bi2Te3 were computed on the scGW QP energies, from the QP database named in the header (KfnQPdb) or from their independent-particle spectra, which coincide with those of the BSE_scGW runs. Concludes that these pairs differ only in the screened interaction, while S3's BSE_G0W0 run used G0W0 energies, so its QP shift must be removed before Δ_W can be isolated.
3. Quantifies eigenvalue self-consistency chain by chain: it opens the direct gap further, by about 0.75–0.81 eV for S3, Te3 and As2S3 but only by about 0.11 eV for Bi2Te3, so the large QP corrections are not an artefact of one-shot G0W0. Explains that the Bi2Te3 scGW gap therefore cannot be obtained by transferring the other chains' increment, and states how it was recovered from Bi2Te3's own archived data.
4. Explains the large binding energies by the weak, nonlocal screening of a thin wire in vacuum, characterized by a polarizability per unit length rather than by the supercell dielectric constant, which only reflects the vacuum fraction. States that the QP gap opening and the binding largely cancel, so that for S3, Te3 and Bi2Te3 the optical gaps lie much closer to the DFT gaps than to the QP gaps, and that the scGW-level W strengthens the binding (Δ_W < 0) by an amount that depends on the chain.
5. Distinguishes bright from dark or weak transitions along the chain. The As2S3 band-edge transition at Γ is nearly dipole-forbidden, so its first bright exciton derives from higher transitions and weak features (1–2% of the peak height) lie below it. Also notes that the band-edge transitions of Te3 and S3 are weak in the independent-particle spectra.
6. Justifies the Bi2Te3 binding energy with its uncertainty: it starts from Bi2Te3's own W0 binding energy and corrects for the W update. Reports that a 1D screened-wire model with the DFT supercell polarizability, raw or calibrated on the other chains, and the mean binding of the other chains overestimate the binding and do not transfer, and that a hydrogenic model built on the supercell dielectric constant has no physical basis.
7. Lists what the data do not support: convergence with k-points, vacuum, cutoff radius and bands (a single setting is archived, and Bi2Te3 used far fewer bands); G0W0+BSE optical gaps for Te3, As2S3 and Bi2Te3 (not archived); the energies of dark excitons, which Haydock spectra do not show; and a precise value of the Bi2Te3 W-update correction.
