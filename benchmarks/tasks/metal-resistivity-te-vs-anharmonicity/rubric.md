# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Uses the converged iterative-BTE tensor (trace/3, inverted to μΩ cm), not the SERTA block printed first; SERTA overestimates ρ by about 7% for Pb and about 20% for Al.
2. Matches numerical settings when isolating an effect. The Al and Nb harmonic runs exist at several fine meshes (Al 50³/60³/70³), while the TE runs use 50³; the Al harmonic reference is therefore the 50³ run (Δ_TE(481 K) ≈ +8.9%). Taking the "converged" 70³ run would add about 2 points of pure mesh error.
3. Explains the mechanisms. Thermal expansion softens the phonons, especially the transverse modes of Pb near melting, which raises the phonon population and the coupling; for Pb the increase is strongly superlinear (ρ_TE/ρ_harm ≈ 1.25, 1.40, 1.60 at 310, 410, 510 K, ≈ 1.9 at 610 K). It also shows, by comparing with a phonon-only estimate, that electronic and deformation-potential changes under expansion add substantially for Pb (rising with T), are small for Nb, and act the other way for Al. TDEP anharmonic renormalization stiffens the phonons and lowers ρ.
4. Shows that the two effects are not separable. The anharmonic correction depends on the lattice: in Pb it is about −25% (410 K) and −29% (610 K) at the 0 K lattice, but about −30% and −48% at the expanded lattice. Combining separately computed TE and anharmonic changes, additively or multiplicatively, overestimates the combined resistivity by 18–60%.
5. Justifies the phonon surrogate used to estimate withheld results, and validates it on released same-lattice pairs (TE+anh vs TE). A transport-weighted kernel (q² from the (1 − cos θ) factor, 1/ω² from |g|² and the occupation) reproduces Pb and Al ratios to 1–3%. The λ-like ⟨ω⁻²⟩ moment is dominated by near-Γ modes and is off by up to about 15%.
6. Describes the metal dependence. In Pb and Al, TE and anharmonicity nearly cancel, so TE+anh ≈ harmonic within a few percent above ~300 K. In Nb, anharmonicity dominates (TE+anh ≈ 0.87–0.89 × harmonic), and the surrogate needs Nb's own calibration because its d-band coupling is not free-electron-like (a Pb/Al calibration is about 7% high).
7. Identifies the regime limits. The 10 K EPW values are grid-limited: 1.57 vs 2.95 μΩ cm for lattices 0.1% apart, far above Bloch–Grüneisen expectations. They must not enter fits or calibrations. The linear-in-T regime holds only above roughly θ_D, about 100 K for Pb.
8. States uncertainties and limits: about 1–3% for surrogate transfers within Pb and Al, about 3–5% for the 610 K TE extrapolation, and the Nb estimate rests on a single calibration point (the Nb 1500 K TE+anh output is missing from the archive). These data cannot separate electronic-structure changes from deformation-potential changes under expansion.
