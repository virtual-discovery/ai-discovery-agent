# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Derives the point group from the structure (the staged CIF is written in P1): D2d with the S4 axis along c and the twofold axes along a and b, mirrors on the diagonal planes. Concludes that the Edelstein (axial–polar) tensor is diagonal with α_yy = −α_xx and α_zz = 0, and that the nonlinear conductivity allows only σ_xyz = σ_xzy = σ_yxz = σ_yzx and σ_zxy = σ_zyx. Notes that in axes rotated by 45° the same crystal would show off-diagonal α_xy = α_yx instead.
2. States that the trace of the gyrotropic tensor (the chirality indicator) vanishes because D2d is achiral although gyrotropic (strongly gyrotropic, achiral class), and relates the trace to the divergence of the k-space moments (no monopole-like texture under S4); a chiral crystal of the same gyrotropy would give a nonzero trace whose sign flips with handedness.
3. Identifies the wave-packet orbital moment with the Berry-phase (modern-theory) moment, gauge invariant and built from interband velocity matrix elements, and rejects the intra-atomic −μ_B⟨L⟩ as the graded quantity: it is a different, on-site contribution that is tens of times smaller and of opposite sign at the valence edge but larger than the spin term at the conduction edge. Notes that the defined moment excludes the intra-atomic part (the diagonal Wannier position operator has no on-site matrix elements), and that adding it would change the conduction-band conclusion; this is discussed as a limitation, not graded.
4. Concludes that orbital moments dominate the valence-band Edelstein response (|α_orb/α_spin| ≈ 10, opposite signs), while spin dominates at the conduction-band edge (|ratio| ≈ 0.3), and attributes the contrast to weak Cu/Se spin–orbit coupling in the valence band versus strong W-d spin–orbit coupling in the conduction band (PDOS).
5. Compares the models with and without spin–orbit coupling at equal hole density, counting two states per band and k-point of the spinless model both in the hole density and in α, and concludes that spin–orbit coupling is not essential for the valence-band orbital response (it reduces it by about 20%). Notes that the header Fermi levels sit mid-gap at different distances from the band edges, so comparing at equal μ on the header scale is not a like-for-like comparison.
6. Documents convergence: the orbital terms converge on moderate meshes, but the small valence-edge spin term drifts with the mesh up to the densest meshes, so the valence-band orbital-to-spin ratio carries a mesh uncertainty of order 10–20% that is stated, not hidden.
7. Attributes the second-order Hall response to the Berry-curvature dipole with D_xx = −D_yy and D_zz = 0, giving σ_zxy = −2σ_xyz, and states that other intrinsic terms (or the full Green-function result) cannot be computed from these data, so their smallness is not established here.
8. States what the data do not support: absolute magnitudes beyond the constant relaxation-time approximation, the intra-atomic orbital contribution, the GGA band gap, and any chirality conclusion beyond the vanishing trace.
