# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. States the energy and exchange conventions (per atom, unit vectors, ordered pairs), and that the released spirals are conical, so their energies scale with sin²θ relative to the Heisenberg pair sums.
2. Identifies configuration sets that cannot separate exchange shells, such as [001] spirals or small supercells whose periodic images make shell sums identical, with an explicit rank, conditioning or aliasing argument.
3. Validates transferability on configurations not used in the fit (another spiral direction or another supercell), and chooses the number of shells from that validation rather than from the in-sample error.
4. Notes that the random states were computed at a different lattice constant (≈2.83 Å) from the spirals (2.87 Å), and assigns shells in each cell's own lattice.
5. Quantifies the breakdown of the small-angle Heisenberg model for large-angle (random) states, giving the size of the energy discrepancy. Attributes it with appropriate caution: longitudinal or itinerant non-Heisenberg effects, with the volume difference as a confound.
6. Explains that long-range exchange tails, and quantities that depend on them (Curie temperature, stiffness from truncated pair sums), are poorly constrained by these data. Does not report a Curie temperature as established.
7. Obtains D from the small-q limit of the data with the cone-angle conversion and q in Å⁻¹ including 2π.
