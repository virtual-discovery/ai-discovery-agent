# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Explains the Peierls mechanism for the half-filled chain (nesting at 2k_F = π/R, a gap opening at the zone boundary of the doubled cell) and recognizes that at fixed mean spacing the dimerization does not stop at a small Peierls distortion but runs to H₂-like molecules (short bond ≈ 0.75 Å), which is why the dimerization energy is of order an eV per atom.
2. Explains the competing spin-density-wave instability with the same 2k_F nesting: on-site correlation grows relative to the bandwidth (U/t) with R, giving an antiferromagnetic (↑↓) solution whose moment and gap grow rapidly with R; and states that a ferromagnetic start relaxes to the nonmagnetic state in this chain, so the magnetic solution needs antiparallel starting moments in a cell of at least two atoms.
3. Shows, with smearing and k-point tests, that the weak antiferromagnetic solution at small R (1.25 Å) is a genuine property of the PBE mean field rather than an artefact: it persists and converges as the smearing is reduced and the k-mesh refined, while large smearing or Fermi–Dirac smearing at 0.005 Ry (an electronic temperature above the mean-field ordering scale) destroys it. Notes that the strongly gapped quantities (dimerized states, magnetism at large R) converge with coarse settings.
4. Describes the coexistence of dimerization and magnetism: the moment decreases with δ and vanishes at a critical dimerization δ_c(R) that grows with R (the spin-density wave survives while the Peierls gap stays below the SDW gap), and states that δ_c(R) is far below δ_eq, so the global PBE minimum at every R from 1.0 to 2.0 Å is the nonmagnetic dimerized chain. Rejects the explanation that magnetism wins at large R.
5. Uses the lowest uniform state (the antiferromagnetic chain) as the reference for the dimerization energy, normalizes energies per atom across cells, and quantifies how much using the nonmagnetic chain would change the answer at large R.
6. Interprets broken-symmetry DFT magnetism as a mean-field representation of static correlation, not literal long-range order: a one-dimensional chain cannot order (Mermin–Wagner, quantum fluctuations; the half-filled Hubbard chain is a Mott insulator with power-law spin correlations).
7. States the limits of the conclusions: fixed collinear 1D geometry with constrained mean spacing, clamped protons with no zero-point motion (important for hydrogen), only the order patterns the cell allows, PBE self-interaction/delocalization error; relates the uniform chain to correlated benchmarks of the hydrogen chain (Motta et al.) rather than claiming PBE settles its ground state.
