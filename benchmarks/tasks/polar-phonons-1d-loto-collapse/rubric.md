# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. States the dimensional fingerprints of the dielectric shift: finite and direction-dependent at Γ in 3D, vanishing linearly in q in 2D, and in 1D ω_LO² − ω_Γ² ∝ q² ln(1/(q t)), so the shift vanishes at Γ with a logarithmic overbending; shows from explicit 1D runs (for example (ω² − ω_Γ²)/q² growing like ln(1/q)) that the data follow the 1D form, and notes that the leading coefficient, 2e²S²/L with S the mode effective charge per √mass, does not depend on the screening or the radius.
2. Identifies the 3D-periodic results as an artefact of periodic images: a finite LO shift for q → 0 (about 100 cm⁻¹ for BN(5,5)) set by the vacuum-diluted ε_zz of the supercell and its volume, decreasing only slowly as the vacuum grows, with 3D and 1D data agreeing once q exceeds about 2π/d; the ph.x Γ frequencies themselves contain no macroscopic field in either code. Uses only 1D data for the isolated-tube predictions.
3. Explains that standard Fourier interpolation of the 1D force constants cannot represent the q² ln q nonanalyticity and gives a much too soft LO at small q; subtracts and re-adds a 1D long-range term with the full kernel (not only its small-qt expansion) and validates the scheme on explicit small-q runs of a released system. Notices that the archived BN(4,4) 1D_code interpolated dispersion disagrees with that tube's explicit 1D runs (it contains no 1D long-range term) and does not use it as a reference.
4. Treats the polarizability per length, A(ε_zz − 1)/(4π) from the cell dielectric tensor, as the physical input and the cylinder radius t and ε_1D as model parameters; fixes t by validation on released tubes or a stated convention, recognizes that the atomic-shell radius underestimates the extent of the electronic polarization, and quantifies how strongly the predictions depend on t for q t of order one.
5. States the regime of validity: the asymptotic q² ln q form and a Γ-only model with a constant short-range part hold only for q t ≪ 1; from about 0.075–0.1 |ΓZ| the mechanical dispersion of the branch matters, which the grid data supply.
6. Tracks the LO branch through its longitudinal mode effective charge (it crosses other branches), and converts the archived q, given in 2π/alat with alat the lateral cell size, to fractions of |ΓZ| = π/L.
7. Discusses spectroscopy: Raman and infrared probes have q ≈ 2π/λ ≪ |ΓZ|, so an isolated tube's LO line lies close to its Γ value, with a shift that depends on radius and wavelength; arrays whose spacing is smaller than the probed wavelength respond like a 3D crystal and recover a finite shift.
8. Quantifies the uncertainty of the predictions (spread of defensible interpolation choices, a few cm⁻¹) and states what the data do not support: absolute agreement with experiment (PBE and pseudopotentials; the authors note too small GaAs Born charges), a uniquely determined effective radius, or the filled-cylinder picture of a hollow tube.
