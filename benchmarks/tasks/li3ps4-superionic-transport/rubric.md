# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Distinguishes the Nernst–Einstein (tracer, Li self-diffusion) and Green–Kubo (collective charge current) conductivities, uses the latter for σ, and attributes H_R < 1 to correlated ion motion, for example positive first-shell Li–Li velocity correlations, quantifying H_R in the superionic state.
2. Locates the superionic transition of each reference from coincident signatures: a step in the PS4 angle fluctuation (onset of discrete flips), a heat-capacity peak and the kink or jump of the γ-started conductivity. Distinguishes it from the higher-temperature heat-capacity peak, which coincides with P diffusion and free PS4 rotation (melting of the PS4 sublattice), and from the gradual onset of measurable conduction.
3. Recognises that the simulations depend on their starting phase: γ-started runs transform above the transition into a mixed β/α-like state, while α-started runs remain a different, more conductive state with lower H_R. Predictions for γ-started runs therefore come from the γ-started superionic branch, and no Arrhenius fit or interpolation crosses the jump.
4. Treats statistical quality explicitly: identifies the Green–Kubo noise floor (about 0.03 S/m, with H_R apparently above 1) in the weakly conducting γ phase, excludes or down-weights those points, and uses the block errors in the fits.
5. Rejects the paddle-wheel picture through a quantitative timescale comparison: in the superionic phase a Li ion hops roughly every picosecond, while a PS4 tetrahedron reorients only every nanosecond or so (ratio of order 10^2.5–10^3). Fast PS4 librations are not reorientations, and the coincident onset of PS4 dynamics and conduction is not evidence of hop-by-hop assistance.
6. Explains that the Li–S term of the current breakdown is frame dependent. In the barycentric frame it is about 27 % of σ, which matches the kinematic recoil of the P/S matrix against the Li centre of mass (from the masses alone). In the matrix frame it is small, so dynamical Li–anion coupling contributes little directly to σ.
7. Links PS4 flipping to the transition, through more available Li sites and a several-fold lower activation energy above the transition. Discusses how the transition temperature depends on the DFT reference (PBEsol < PBE0 < r2SCAN), argues which reference is most credible, and does not equate a simulated transition temperature or extrapolated room-temperature conductivity with experiment without caveats (cell size, time scale, metastability).
