# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Obtains a0 from the zero of the in-plane stress (or an energy minimum at a converged cutoff) with the S height relaxed, converts the cell-averaged stress of the slab to a 2D stress (or uses only its sign and zero), and shows that the stress is converged with the cutoff: the 35 Ry cutoff, although adequate for band-energy differences, carries a Pulay stress that shrinks a0 by about 1 %, which would shift every strain.
2. Identifies the two competing edges and their characters: under tension the valence state at Γ (Mo d_z² with S p_z, out of plane) rises above the K valence state (Mo d_xy/d_x²−y²) and the gap becomes indirect Γ→K; under compression the conduction minimum moves from K (Mo d_z²) to the Q (Λ) valleys roughly midway along Γ–K, which is not a high-symmetry point and must be located by sampling the Γ–K line rather than only Γ, K and M (a 9×9 mesh, for example, misses it).
3. Distinguishes relaxed-ion from clamped-ion strain: the S height decreases under tension and increases under compression, the out-of-plane Γ and Q states are sensitive to it, and keeping the unstrained height moves both ends of the direct-gap range and changes the deformation potentials by tens of per cent; states how the scalar-relativistic relaxed geometry was carried into the spin–orbit calculations.
4. Quantifies spin–orbit coupling at each competing state: the K valence band splits by about 0.15 eV (upper branch raised by about half), the Γ valence state is essentially unsplit, the Q conduction states split by several tens of meV (lower branch lowered), the K conduction band only by a few meV; concludes that SOC moves the tensile end from near zero strain to about +0.6 % and also shifts the compressive end, so a correction applied only to the K valence band is insufficient.
5. Explains that raw Kohn–Sham eigenvalues of a periodic slab are referred to the cell-averaged electrostatic potential, which moves with the in-plane area (and with the cell height), and refers band energies to the vacuum plateau of the planar-averaged electrostatic potential for the absolute deformation potentials; reports that the vacuum-referenced edges do not depend on the cell height in the offered range.
6. Treats uniaxial stress correctly: finds the transverse strain from zero transverse stress with relaxed atoms (including the in-plane S shift along armchair allowed by the reduced symmetry), and recognises that uniaxial strain splits the six Q valleys into the pair along the strain axis and the four at ±60°, samples both families, and locates the CBM in the pair along k_x.
7. States the limits: PBE underestimates the quasiparticle gap and there are no excitonic effects, so the absolute gap and the optical transition differ from experiment (optical gap about 1.9 eV); the crossover strains are relative to this protocol's a0 (experiment 3.16 Å) and are sensitive to the functional, to substrate and to temperature; no phonon renormalisation.
