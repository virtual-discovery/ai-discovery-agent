# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Identifies the top valence bands of unstrained GaN as two levels that are heavy along c (HH, LH; nearly degenerate, about 7 meV apart) above a crystal-field level that is light along c (about 56 meV lower), using the GW band data. States that compressing c raises the crystal-field level to the top between 1% and 2% compression, and that this reordering, not a change of phonons, causes the jump of μ_zz. Tension along c leaves the order and μ unchanged.
2. Explains in-plane uniaxial and shear strains by the splitting of HH and LH. A band that is light along the compressed principal direction moves to the top, so the mobility rises along that direction. Notes that shear rotates the principal axes of μ, and that the largest in-plane mobility is along a principal direction, not along x or y.
3. States that the mirror planes of 6mm make the diagonal of μ even and the strained off-diagonal element odd in shear strain, so compress and expand shear states are mirror images. Quantifies the residual numerical differences between mirror pairs (a few percent).
4. States that e1↔e2 (and e4↔e5) are related by x↔y only at linear order, since a 90° rotation is not a symmetry of 6mm. Shows from the data that the swap fails at 2–3% strain by about 10–20%.
5. States that a linear piezomobility tensor does not describe the data even at ±1%. The response is non-analytic at zero strain, because HH and LH are closer than kT. It is one-sided for c-axis strain and saturates at 2–3% once the splitting exceeds a few kT.
6. Explains why SERTA is not used: the IBTE/SERTA ratio varies from about 2 to 3.5 with the band character, and SERTA mis-ranks the strains for μ_zz (e4 above e3). Notes that IBTE runs flagged as unconverged after 1000 iterations have negligible residual changes.
7. Gives a design recommendation with magnitudes. Among single 2% strains, c-axis compression gives μ_zz ≈ 3.4× the unstrained value, and in-plane shear gives the largest in-plane mobility, ≈ 3.5×, along a direction near the compressive principal axis. Describes the temperature dependence: the enhancement shrinks with temperature (for c-axis compression about 4× at 100 K, 3.4× at 300 K and 2.4× at 500 K, since the strained mobilities fall faster, about T^-2.5, than the unstrained one, about T^-2). States that the archive's shear labels carry ε_ij = X%, an engineering strain of 2X%.
8. Lists what the data do not support: quantitative μ at 3% c-axis compression beyond a bracket, the exact crossing strain, combined strains, and DFT-versus-GW ordering effects.
