# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Gives the per-Ni energies of the collinear configurations in the stated convention (FM: E0 + 6J1 + 3J2; AFM-II: E0 − 3J2; AFM-I: E0 − 2J1 + 3J2, or an equivalent set such as a 3:1 ferrimagnet in the cubic cell), states that FM and AFM-II alone determine only J1 + J2, identifies which offered cell realizes AFM-II ([111] planes: the rhombohedral cell) and AFM-I ([001] planes: the tetragonal cell), and normalizes the per-cell energies per formula unit.
2. Takes each energy difference between configurations of the same cell and k mesh (or shows, with numbers, that absolute energies per formula unit from different cells agree at the meshes used), and explains why: the k-sampling error of an absolute energy per formula unit at moderate meshes is several meV, comparable to 8|J1|, so mixing cells silently biases J1 even when each same-cell difference looks converged.
3. Demonstrates k-point convergence of each difference with a mesh ladder, notes that convergence is slower at the smaller U (smaller FM and AFM-I gaps) than at the larger U, and states that the differences are insensitive to the cutoff and to small smearing in the gapped states.
4. Explains the mechanism: J2 is the 180° Ni–O–Ni superexchange (Anderson/Kramers) and dominates, J1 is weak and ferromagnetic (90° path, Goodenough–Kanamori), and both fall with U roughly as t²/U_eff because U widens the charge-transfer/Mott gap; quantifies the change between U = 4 and 8 eV; treats U as a protocol parameter rather than a fitted truth (the experimental J2 ≈ 19 meV or a linear-response U would select a value; agreement at one U is not validation).
5. Derives the molecular-field Néel temperature with S(S+1) for S = 1 and explains why J1 drops out for AFM-II (six parallel and six antiparallel nearest neighbours), and states that mean field overestimates T_N for the fcc lattice (quantum and thermal fluctuations; experiment ≈ 523 K) and that the rhombohedral exchange striction below T_N is not modelled.
6. Characterizes the small-U regime with evidence: in plain PBE the FM state is a metal whose moment and energy depend on the k mesh and smearing (needing dense meshes, best in the 1-f.u. cell, and the zero-smearing limit), small starting moments give a different low-moment solution or SCF failure, the FM gap only opens near U ≈ 2 eV (located with meshes that contain the band extrema; shifted meshes report a spurious gap), and the Ni moments are no longer rigid between configurations; concludes that a Heisenberg description with fixed J's is not valid at U = 0 and that U = 0 must not be used in a J(U) fit.
7. Checks for and rules out metastable DFT+U occupation states at the graded U (different starting moments give the same energy; the Ni 3d occupation eigenvalues show the expected t2g⁶e_g² high-spin d⁸ configuration in every configuration), and states the limits: fixed lattice parameter, collinear spins without spin–orbit coupling, Dudarev U with non-orthogonalized atomic projectors and these pseudopotentials, only J1 and J2, and no magnon or beyond-mean-field T_N calculation.
