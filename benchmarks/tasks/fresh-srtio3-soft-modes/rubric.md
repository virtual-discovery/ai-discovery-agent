# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Builds the zone-centre force-constant matrix from displacements of all inequivalent atoms (not a single "Ti mode"), imposes the acoustic sum rule (translational invariance) and diagonalizes the mass-weighted matrix; reports the soft-mode eigenvector as a mixture of Ti and O-cage motion.
2. Shows that the soft-mode frequency, ε∞ and Z* need denser k meshes than the total energy (gives the mesh ladder), and that the frozen-phonon amplitude is small enough for a harmonic estimate of an unstable mode.
3. Obtains the LO frequencies from the nonanalytic (macroscopic-field) term with ε∞ (not ε0) and charge-neutral Born charges, applied to the full polar block (all polar modes mix), and notes the anomalously large Ti and O_∥ Born charges.
4. Explains that the R-point rotation cannot appear in the five-atom cell, maps it in the doubled cell, and references the tilt energies to the undistorted doubled cell on the same k mesh; reports energies per formula unit.
5. Explains the PBE lattice-parameter sensitivity: the protocol's a0 exceeds experiment, the polar instability grows strongly with volume, so the polar result is protocol-specific; it does not claim SrTiO3 is ferroelectric.
6. Distinguishes the Γ (polar) and R (antiferrodistortive) order parameters, connects the tilt to the 105 K cubic-tetragonal transition, and states that the two instabilities compete (biquadratic coupling; tilts suppress the polar instability), so their energy gains are not additive.
7. Discusses quantum paraelectricity: zero-point motion can suppress a shallow (few meV/f.u.) polar well, so a DFT instability need not imply ferroelectric order.
8. Explains the strain effect through strain-polarization coupling and the Poisson relaxation of c under biaxial tension (in-plane mode softens, out-of-plane mode hardens).
