# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. States the classical energy convention (unit vectors or spins, sum over the listed entries with each bond listed from both ends), the spin length used for the dynamics, and shows with the purchased dispersion data that the chosen normalisation is right while a doubled Hamiltonian or the free-ion spin 5/2 is not.
2. Finds the ground state by energy minimisation from several starting states in the k = 0 orthorhombic cell (justified by magnon cones centred on integer (H,K,L)), and describes it as noncollinear: Mn1 moments perpendicular-ish to canted Mn2a moments, with the global orientation fixed by anisotropy.
3. Computes spin waves with noncollinear linear spin-wave theory in local rotated frames, including neutron weights (perpendicular projection, form factor), and explains that at high energy the (0,3,Ql) intensity follows the steep upper branch rather than the acoustic branch.
4. Models the measurement: energy and momentum resolution of the constant-energy scans and the two-Gaussian extraction of branch positions; does not compare bare dispersion curves with fitted peak positions.
5. Concludes that Mn1 carries an ordered but weakly coupled, field-susceptible moment, citing evidence (KKR moment, the field-induced transitions inside AFM1, the Mn1–Mn2 optical mode), and notes that the Mn1 couplings are not first-principles values.
6. Compares the exchange tables (noncollinear vs collinear reference, 4.5 Å vs 17 Å range) against the data and states that they are not cleanly distinguished; notes direction-dependent misfits (h and k prefer different energy scales, the k plateau near 2.75 r.l.u.).
7. States what the data do not support: individual Mn1 couplings, the full anisotropy tensor, the long-range exchange tail, and the soft AFM2 (80 K) spectra, which none of these models reproduces.
