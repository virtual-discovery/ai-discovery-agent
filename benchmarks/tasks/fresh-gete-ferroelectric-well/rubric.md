# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Describes the R3m distortion as a polar Ge/Te displacement along [111] (the zone-centre T1u mode of rocksalt) coupled to a rhombohedral shear of the cell along the same axis and to the volume; states that symmetry forbids a bilinear coupling between the polar mode and strain in the cubic phase but allows the coupling η·P² (η the [111] shear or the volume strain), so the strain renormalizes the quartic, not the harmonic, term.
2. Separates the clamped-cell depth (displacement alone at the rocksalt cell) from the fully relaxed depth with numbers from both, gives the fraction supplied by the strain coupling, and concludes that the instability is a proper polar one (the clamped cell is already unstable) strongly deepened by strain, rather than a strain-driven (improper) one or one that exists only after relaxation.
3. Compares the two phases each at its own zero-pressure structure (equal pressure, equal enthalpy reference), not at a common volume or at an experimental cell, and shows that the relaxed R3m structure is a stationary point (residual stresses and forces, or an energy minimum in a, α and x).
4. Identifies that rocksalt GeTe has only a small direct gap at L in PBE, so its total energy converges slowly with the k mesh (coarse meshes overstate the well depth), and that under compression the gap at L closes, so the soft-mode force constant near the critical pressure must be computed for a semimetal: fixed occupations are invalid there and smearing width and mesh density must be converged.
5. Obtains the critical pressure from the sign change of the harmonic force constant at the rocksalt site (small displacements, or forces, rather than energies at large displacements that include the quartic term), with pressures taken consistently from the same calculations; discusses whether the transition is continuous (the sign of the strain-renormalized quartic coefficient) and does not equate the soft-mode pressure with a measured transition pressure.
6. Isolates the spin–orbit effect with a scalar-relativistic and a fully relativistic pseudopotential pair of the same family at fixed structures, and explains that comparing a spin–orbit calculation with the `sssp` result would mix the pseudopotential difference (Ge 3d frozen versus in valence) into the apparent spin–orbit effect; gives the size and sign of the change.
7. Connects the instability to resonant p-bonding (half-filled p bands, Peierls-like dimerization along [111] that opens the gap at L) and explains why compression, which broadens the bands and closes the gap, suppresses it.
8. States the protocol limits and the comparison with experiment: PBE volume overestimate, static lattice (no zero-point or thermal effects), no spin–orbit coupling in the protocol; the well depth per formula unit is not a transition temperature (the ~700 K ferroelectric transition and the paired GAP/TDEP study's picture involve anharmonic fluctuations), and the soft-mode pressure is not the experimental transition pressure.
