# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Identifies the volume-mediated (elastic) H–H attraction as the driver of the separation. Supports this with evidence that fixed-cell (fixed-volume) energetics give only weak short-range interactions and no gap, while zero-stress relaxation produces the attraction. Rejects short-range chemical attraction as the main cause.
2. Explains what limits the β phase at high x (the H–H interaction turns repulsive as the Pd d-band holes fill, so Δe(x) curves upward) and why the gap closes at a critical temperature. Does not attribute the gap to band filling alone.
3. Shows that the dilute quantities were converged with supercell size at zero pressure. States that small periodic cells carry a large elastic image interaction of the H dilatation, which biases the solution energy (lower at zero pressure, higher at fixed volume).
4. Treats the H/D nuclei quantum-mechanically beyond the harmonic approximation for the soft, anharmonic octahedral site, for example by solving the nuclear Schrödinger equation on a frozen-host energy grid. Quantifies how far the harmonic zero-point energy is off.
5. Determines the site preference with and without zero-point energy: O is preferred, and the T sites' larger ZPE widens the gap. Gives the resulting small T-site occupancy.
6. Explains the isotope effect: an inverse Sieverts isotope effect, with H more soluble than D (K_H/K_D > 1), arising because the in-metal ZPE difference is smaller than half the gas-phase one. Includes the excited-state (thermal) vibrational free energy at 300 K, and states that the plateau pressure of D is correspondingly higher.
7. Explains the coherent versus incoherent critical temperature. Coherency strain lowers T_c, through Cahn's criterion with the elastically soft <100> direction of the anisotropic Pd crystal and the Vegard coefficient.
8. Validates the potential against the archived DFT records (for example Pd, PdH, H₂ and PdH_x cells) before relying on it, and states the size of the residual errors.
9. States the limits: mean-field ideal entropy (no short-range order), static T_c, x-independent H vibrations, PBE-level energetics. States that the isotope dependence of T_c is not resolved, and that the absolute numbers are model-specific and not experimental values.
