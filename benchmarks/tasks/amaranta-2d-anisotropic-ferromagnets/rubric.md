# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. States the energy convention used for the mapping (unit vectors, each pair counted once, on-site term per site) and applies it consistently to the anisotropy energies, the Monte Carlo and the predictions.
2. Finds that CrSiSe3 is not a ferromagnet in these data: its nearest-neighbour and third-neighbour exchange are antiferromagnetic (about +5 and +1.9 meV), so the classical ground state is the Néel state. Evaluates its anisotropy for the Néel pattern, in which the nearest-neighbour exchange anisotropy changes sign relative to the ferromagnet: moments in plane, about −0.11 meV/Cr, against out-of-plane +0.03 meV/Cr if a ferromagnet is assumed.
3. Explains that ferromagnetic configurations alone cannot separate single-ion from exchange anisotropy, because both are quadratic forms of the moment direction. Separates them with configurations that reverse the nearest-neighbour products (Néel versus ferromagnet; antiferromagnetic chains along a for CrSBr), or with single-site rotations designed to beat the noise, and gives the split per compound: single-ion dominated for CrCl3 (0.028 of 0.034 meV); comparable parts in CrBr3 and CrI3 (0.08 + 0.08 and 0.21 + 0.21 meV); in CrGeTe3 an easy-plane single-ion term (−0.19 meV) overridden by exchange anisotropy (+0.71 meV). Connects the exchange part to ligand spin–orbit coupling (Cl < Br < I; Te heavy).
4. CrI3: shows that the three bond tensors differ (related by the C3 rotation about Cr, with J(−d) = J(d)ᵀ) and that copying one bond's tensor to all bonds would wrongly put the moments in the plane. Obtains the off-diagonal elements from noncollinear configurations, and decomposes the bond in the ideal octahedral frame: J ≈ −4.15, K ≈ +1.23, Γ ≈ 0.09, Γ′ ≈ −0.11 meV. Takes the side of the frame from the I positions; the mirror-image frame would give K ≈ −0.14 meV.
5. Ordering temperature: isotropic exchange alone gives no order at T > 0 in 2D (Mermin–Wagner). The anisotropy gap makes the transition Ising-like, and Tc depends on it only logarithmically. Obtains Tc of the CrI3 model (about 35 K) from classical Monte Carlo with a finite-size analysis (Binder-cumulant crossing or equivalent), states that mean field overestimates it (about 62 K), and that the further-neighbour ferromagnetic J2 matters. Does not claim reliable ordering temperatures for CrCl3 and CrBr3, whose anisotropies are at or below the source's precision.
6. CrSBr: identifies a DM vector along b (about 0.26 meV) on the Cr chains along a, staggered between the two chains (related by inversion), so it cancels in the ferromagnet and in co-rotating spirals. Explains that a cell two lattice constants long along a measures J + Jᵀ and loses it, and that reading J_xz as symmetric exchange would tilt the moments. Finds triaxial anisotropy in these data (easy c, intermediate a, hard b; about 0.063 and 0.118 meV/Cr), and a zone-centre gap (2/S)√(Δ1Δ2) ≈ 0.115 meV rather than a uniaxial value.
7. Notes that these energies contain no magnetic dipole–dipole interaction. For a 3 μB honeycomb layer the shape anisotropy (about 0.05 meV/Cr, favouring the plane) exceeds the magnetocrystalline anisotropy of CrCl3 (0.034 meV/Cr), so the real monolayer is likely easy-plane, while CrBr3 and CrI3 keep their out-of-plane axis. The out-of-plane axis of this CrSBr model differs from the in-plane b axis reported experimentally, and dipolar terms or method dependence could account for it.
8. States the limits: the energies come from the authors' fitted model rather than new DFT; anisotropies of 0.01–0.1 meV are near the source precision; quantum fluctuations, the S = 3/2 spin-wave convention and interlayer coupling are not addressed by these data.
