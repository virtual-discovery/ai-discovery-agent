# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Uses a statistically controlled Green–Kubo estimator (cepstral/spectral analysis with a justified cutoff, or a running integral with a stated noise floor) and quotes error bars; recognises that the LAMMPS heat flux is the extensive J·V and normalises by the volume and the run's mean temperature (NVE runs sit below their nominal temperature).
2. Recognises that once the anions diffuse the heat flux couples to a non-vanishing mass (anion) flux, so the single-flux Green–Kubo integral of the raw flux depends on the arbitrary per-species energy zero; removes the coupling with the multicomponent (Onsager) formula, κ = [S⁻¹]₀₀⁻¹ of the heat/anion-current spectral matrix (or an equivalent decorrelation). Quantifies the correction: negligible in the crystal, about 1.2× in superionic UO₂, about 2× in CaF₂ and about 4× in PbF₂ at the highest temperatures.
3. Explains the temperature dependence: κ falls in the crystal but much more slowly than the phonon-gas 1/T law and saturates at a temperature-independent value above the transition (UO₂ ≈ 1.2, CaF₂ ≈ 1.35, PbF₂ ≈ 0.65 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ in 768-atom cells); attributes the plateau to strongly scattered, diffusive (Allen–Feldman-like) vibrational heat carriers in the anion-disordered lattice, not to a convective contribution of the moving ions (that one is removed by the multicomponent treatment, and keeping it would produce a spurious rise of κ above the transition).
4. Quantifies the cell-size effect: 96-atom cells underestimate κ substantially (about 25% for crystalline UO₂), 324 atoms still about 15% low for crystalline UO₂, and the size effect shrinks in the superionic regime; uses 768-atom cells for the graded states and does not claim a thermodynamic-limit κ from three cell sizes.
5. Tests the ensemble: compares NVT (global CSVR thermostat) with NVE runs of the same state and finds agreement within error, so NVT runs are usable (needed for PbF₂ 4×4×4 above 900 K and for CaF₂ below 900 K, where no NVE runs exist).
6. States what the data do not support: thermodynamic-limit κ, a sharp saturation temperature (the onset is only located to within a few hundred kelvin), and comparison with experiment for these fixed-volume empirical force fields.
