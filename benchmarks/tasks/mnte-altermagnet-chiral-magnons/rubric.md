# Physical-reasoning rubric

Each item is a checkable statement that the report must make explicitly.

1. Identifies the 9.83 Å same-sublattice bonds (in-plane √3a combined with ±c) as the shortest shell that symmetry splits into two classes exchanged between the sublattices (the Mn sites are related by the 6₃ screw or a mirror, not by inversion or translation). States that the shorter same-sublattice shells (4.15, 6.71, 7.18, 7.89 and 8.30 Å) stay single classes.
2. Gives the splitting form factor, ∝ sin(2πl)[sin 2π(2h+k) + sin 2π(k−h) − sin 2π(h+2k)] or an equivalent. Names the nodal planes (l = 0 and l = ½, and the (h h l)-type planes) and the g-wave character. Places the maximum near (⅓, 0, ¼) or an equivalent point.
3. Distinguishes the ~4 meV branch difference at the zone centre (easy-plane single-ion anisotropy, linearly polarized modes) from the altermagnetic splitting, and does not report the zone centre as the point of maximal splitting.
4. Notices that the IN20 headers use lattice constants (a = 4.17 Å, c = 6.87 Å) different from MnTe's, and converts the nominal (h, 0, 1.25) to the true l ≈ 1.22 (with or without rescaling h) before predicting the peaks.
5. Obtains |J_a − J_b| from the measured branch separations, with the energy of each HYSPEC pixel fixed by the kinematics. Quantifies the spread between cuts and instruments, and does not treat the value as more precise than that spread.
6. Explains that the chiral term is proportional to the projection of the domain-averaged Néel vector on Q. Explains that the y-channel nuclear–magnetic interference at (1 0 ±1) measures the a*-projection relevant to (h 0 l) wave vectors, while the z channel measures the b projection.
7. Transfers the chirality calibration between crystals and states through the sign of the nuclear–magnetic interference. Notes that both the interference term and the splitting form factor change sign with l, so the instrument frames (l > 0 on IN20, l < 0 on HYSPEC) do not change the rule.
8. Explains switching: field cooling selects the antiphase-domain balance, so reversing the field reverses n̄ and every chiral sign. Rules out Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya splitting (Mn sites are inversion centres) and magnon–phonon mixing as the origin of opposite-sign chiral peaks that follow the domain state.
9. States what the data do not determine: the sign of J_a − J_b (which class is stronger) without an absolute domain reference, the absolute domain imbalance (instrument polarization factors differ), and the individual isotropic exchanges, which come from the prior.
